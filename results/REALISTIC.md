# Real-map illustration

Generated by `run_realistic.py`. This is an illustration, not company data.

- 25 customer regions: the largest US metro areas, demand = 2020 population / 100,000 (1,393 units in total).
- 8 candidate hubs picked by us, each with capacity 348 units (25% of demand) and an assumed opening cost of 1,500 (made-up dollars). Shipping rate 0.01 per unit per km, great-circle distances.

| Plan | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Fair baseline, all hubs open (shipping) | 5,249 |
| LP, all hubs open (shipping) | 5,194 |
| LP, all hubs open (shipping + opening) | 17,194 |
| MILP chooses hubs (shipping + opening) | 13,813 |

- LP saves 1.0% of shipping versus the fair baseline.
- MILP opens 5 of 8 hubs: Reno, Dallas hub, Chicago hub, Atlanta hub, Harrisburg. It saves 19.7% of total cost versus opening all.

The default opening cost was chosen up front, but a few values were tried before settling on it, so treat the sweep as the honest picture of how much the hub choice depends on it.
The LP saves little over the fair baseline here because capacity is generous (2x demand overall), so 'nearest hub with room left' is already close to optimal. Savings depend on how tight capacity is.
